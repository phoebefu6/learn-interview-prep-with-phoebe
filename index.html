Learn Interview Prep with Phoebe

The candidate side of data and AI interviews. An interview loop is a set of measurements, and the job description says what each round is measuring. Six sessions on preparing for exactly that: reading the job into competencies, building a story bank that covers them, the SQL round and its traps, the statistics and product case rounds, take-home assignments, the questions you ask, and a review after every round. One constructed candidate, Ren, an analyst moving into data science, and one bench: six SQL questions run for real in your browser on 2,085 orders, and a story-bank coverage matrix.

The six sessions 🧭 · from the job description to the review after the last round

The running case is Ren, a constructed analyst with three years of dashboards, SQL and A/B test readouts, applying for a first data scientist role in product analytics. Ren, both job descriptions and every story are invented for teaching, and every page says so. No real employer's process is described, and nothing here is about pay.

🔎 Session 1 · foundations

What the interview is actually measuring

A loop as a set of measurements, what selection research says structured interviews predict and where it disagrees, what structure means from the candidate's chair, and a job description read into a prep plan.

▶ Start here 🗂️ Session 2 · core

A story bank that covers the job

Past-behaviour questions, STAR and where it comes from, eight stories tagged against every competency they honestly evidence, trimmed to finish, and told without overselling.

STAR, tagged 🧮 Session 3 · core

The SQL round

Read the question before typing: the grain of a join, NULLs in an average, WHERE against HAVING, date ranges, the id that is not a person. And thinking aloud while someone watches.

four checks first ⏱️ Session 4 · bench night

The practice-round bench

Six SQL questions on real orders with a strict checker that prints the first wrong row, then Ren's story bank against a job description, the one-story anti-lever and a break button.

★ the bench 📈 Session 5 · deeper

Stats, experiments and the product case

A p-value explained in one sentence, why stopping a test early is unreliable, designing an experiment out loud, choosing a metric and estimating a number in a product case.

reasoning out loud 📝 Session 6 · deeper

Take-homes, your questions, and the review after

A time-boxed take-home with its assumptions written down, the questions worth asking and when, and a review after every round that feeds the bank and the drill list.

What is honest here 🧾

Interview advice travels with famous numbers, and some of them do not come from where they are said to. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus

"Structured interviews predict performance better than unstructured ones"Read at source in both the 1998 and 2022 estimates; how far the numbers should be corrected is contested, and session 1 teaches the disagreement
"Interviewers decide in the first four minutes"Not what the study found. Session 1 traces it
